# Experimental overtime timeline

`packages/core/src/overtime.ts` supplies a separate three-outcome timeline estimator. It does not run the regulation WP model in overtime and does not extend coaching or ruling counterfactuals. Estimates remain **experimental and uncalibrated**; a 20-game support threshold is a minimum coverage rule, not proof of accuracy.

The reference contains actual 2017–2025 regular-season overtime states. Sources, licenses and SHA-256 hashes are embedded in `models/overtime-reference.json`. Build it with `node --import tsx scripts/build-overtime-reference.ts`; `--snapshot-root` can point to the existing content-addressed source cache. Missing public CSVs are downloaded once into ignored `data/overtime-sources`. Current-season games never enter a target's reference set. `models/overtime-evaluation.json` records chronological 2024 and 2025 diagnostics. The compact 2026 test fixture is for prospective regression only.

Rule phases follow [2026 NFL Rule 16](https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/league/tqivdkzt9mu6wdgsh1ku.pdf), with the regular-season first-possession change applied from 2025. Only ordinary ten-minute regular-season overtime is modeled. The parser uses source order and already-observed events; possession, score, period clock, down/distance, field position and timeouts describe the current preplay state. Future drive summaries, final scores and provider WP are not predictors. Historical terminal scores are outcome labels only.

Opening possessions before 2025 are incompatible with opening possessions under the new rule. Replies to an opening field goal and tied sudden-death states can be compared across the permitted seasons. Touchdown responses, tries, postseason continuation, ambiguous possession changes, onside/muffed kicks, blocked kicks and safety branches remain unavailable. The explicit `END GAME` record can carry an **observed** win/loss/tie result; it is not a model estimate. A terminal tie has win probabilities zero and tie probability one.

For each target state, compare only its exact phase and score differential. Take at most one closest state from each prior game, within five minutes, 35 field yards and one down; standardized distance must be at most three. Distance combines clock/180, field/20, down difference, log(1+yards-to-go) difference and each timeout difference/2. Select at most 40 games with equal influence and withhold estimates below 20 games. Win/loss/tie counts receive a symmetric 0.5 pseudocount per outcome, so finite empirical samples are never arbitrarily converted to certainty. These fixed choices were not tuned against the 2026 examples.

Evaluation uses each test year's strictly prior seasons. Three-class Brier is the sum of squared errors across win/loss/tie, range 0–2; average first within each game, then equally across games with estimates. The phase baseline is evaluated on the same estimated test states and takes one earliest phase/score-compatible observation per prior game. Terminal observed results are excluded. Coverage and errors must be reported together: 2025's new opening-possession and touchdown-response phases have little or no support, and results for a handful of modeled games do not establish calibration or performance on withheld states.

The frozen reference has 2,136 eligible states from 131 of the 132 regular-season OT games in its sources. Chronological 2024 evaluation covers 180/200 scrimmage entries in 15 games, with game-weighted Brier 0.3474 versus 0.4413 for the phase baseline. The 2025 check covers 50/216 entries in six games, with Brier 0.2904 versus 0.4605. These are descriptive diagnostics, not a calibration or deployment-promotion gate. Only 14 prior games use the new opening rule, below the minimum of20. The estimator pools historical team strengths and environments; matching OT scoring/entitlement rules does not remove changes in kickoff rules or league play. No parameter was adjusted to improve the GB–NYJ or IND–KC examples.

No estimate identifies why a play occurred, adjudicates a call, or establishes intent. Probabilities may move non-monotonically between adjacent states because their finite historical comparison sets differ.
