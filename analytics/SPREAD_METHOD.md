# Recorded spread versus final result

The comparison uses `spread_line` from each report’s immutable nflverse schedule snapshot. The [nflreadr schedule dictionary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nflverse/nflreadr/main/data-raw/dictionary_schedules.csv) defines a positive value as a home favorite and `result` as home points minus away points. The [PBP dictionary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nflverse/nflreadr/main/data-raw/dictionary_pbp.csv) describes the matching field as a closing line and credits Pro-Football-Reference. The schedule row does not name a bookmaker or provide a quote timestamp. The displayed value is the recorded closing spread, not a live offer or a claim to cover every bookmaker.

`expectedHomeMargin = spread_line`; `actualHomeMargin = homeScore − awayScore`; `homeMarginError = actualHomeMargin − expectedHomeMargin`; `absoluteError = abs(homeMarginError)`. Positive error means the home team covered, negative means the away team covered, and zero is a push. Zero **line** is pick’em. Missing or invalid lines stay unavailable. Final scores include overtime; no regulation-only score is substituted. An underdog winning, a cover, and a push are separate descriptive fields, not automatic suspicion flags.

## Frozen reference and rating rule

`analytics/models/spread-reference.json` contains one observation for each of 7,276 completed 1999–2025 regular-season and postseason games. It was derived offline from the existing archived [nflverse schedules release](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/download/schedules/games.csv). No eligible row lacked a score or spread. The artifact records source URL, retrieval time, original CSV SHA-256, row checksum, season counts, and license. Data attribution is Lee Sharpe / nflverse, CC BY 4.0; transformations are field selection and completed-season filtering. The original compressed CSV remains in `analytics/models/game-profile-sources` at the checksum-named path recorded in the artifact. Rebuild with `node analytics/build-spread-reference.mjs`; no network access is used.

For a target season, only strictly earlier seasons enter the denominator. The target season and future games are excluded even when present in the artifact. Count prior games whose absolute error is **equal to or larger than** the target error, then divide by the number of eligible prior games. This inclusive tail frequency is conservative at ties. The displayed percentile is `100 × (1 − tail frequency)` and therefore means the percentage of prior errors that were strictly smaller. It is not a predicted probability of this game or a p-value.

At least 500 prior games are required for a market rating contribution. A tail frequency of at most 10% supplies a floor of **Debatable (2)**; at most 5% supplies **Hmm (3)**. Otherwise the market supplies no increase. These are declared product screening thresholds, not trained coefficients or independently validated misconduct thresholds. The overall rating uses the larger of its existing tier and this floor; it does not add correlated signals. Market surprise alone never raises a game to Sus (4) or RIGGED? (5). Existing missing-data rules still leave the overall game Unrated even when a market comparison is available.

The reference pools eras, favorite sizes, venues, game types, and overtime rules. It measures an unconditional final-margin discrepancy, not a spread-conditioned forecast, a bookmaker’s risk, or wagering returns. Ordinary football variation, injuries, weather, coaching, and late scores can all cause large discrepancies. No betting behavior, money flow, call correctness, or intent is inferred.

## Immutable upgrades

Game audit v4 adds market context without rerunning R or changing stored regulation/OT estimates and statistical metrics. Refresh reads the original checksum-addressed schedule bytes, validates the complete saved game against that row, and retains every source snapshot. Missing/corrupt/mismatched evidence fails without replacing the existing report. Future source changes belong to normal analysis reconciliation. Source/reference hashes and market model version enter the analysis fingerprint. Bundle imports recompute both market arithmetic and historical counts, and reject modified results even when the outer bundle checksum has been recalculated.
